A design may look great on a monitor but lose clarity and impact when scaled down for a website icon, social media profile picture, or business card. That is why I never judge a logo by its first impression alone.

A professional logo should be legible, scalable, distinctive, and versatile. It should remain recognisable as a small icon while still representing the brand effectively on signage, website headers, or printed documents. Colour, shape, spacing, and logo variations are all carefully considered throughout the logo design process.

In many projects, the logo is the first step in building a broader visual identity. Once it has been finalised, the next stage is to create or align the other brand elements, from the colour palette and typography to Business Cards Portfolio, letterheads, social media templates, and other touchpoints. When these elements follow a shared visual logic, the brand feels more consistent, credible, and trustworthy.

The choice of logo style should not depend only on personal taste or short-lived trends. A wordmark may be the right solution for a brand with a distinctive, memorable name, while a symbol may work better for a business that needs to be recognised quickly. In some cases, combining both creates the strongest and most flexible result.

A good logo has to work in real conditions: on light and dark backgrounds, in colour and monochrome, at small and large sizes, and across digital and print applications. That is why the final deliverables are usually prepared in the required variations and formats, so they can be used confidently on your website, social media, printed materials, official documents, and advertising.
